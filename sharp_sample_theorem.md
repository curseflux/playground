# Sample size versus protected bits: a sharp uniform law, and its limitation

## Executive conclusion

The earlier factor `d log q` is not necessary for a useful **uniform** certificate bound. It can be removed by bounding the largest two-coordinate displacement that any quantization cell can contain, without enumerating originals.

However, the hoped-for rapid shrinkage of both aliases and column conflicts is false for the full-grid, overparameterized Gaussian-feature model under the previous uniform guarantee. With high probability **every transcript generated by a sufficiently interior original** has a complete column-conflict graph. Its aliases persist up to a scale proportional to `W/(Delta sqrt(log(Nd)))`. This is not merely the existence of one unusually selected transcript.

In a stated high-resolution regime, the resulting matching bounds give

\[
\boxed{L^*_{\max}(H,\xi)=2\log_2(W/\Delta)-\log_2\ln N+O(1).}
\]

The same matching law holds for each such interior-generated transcript's **uniform-fiber** budget. It is not the fixed-original law `2 log_2(W/(N Delta))`. The distinction is mathematical, not computational. A separate proposition below recovers the faster dependence, up to logarithmic factors, under a per-original probability guarantee that does not promise uniform recovery of that original's entire fiber. Neither result is claimed here for BF16 arithmetic or arbitrary trained neural features.

The arguments below are newly derived for this project; they have not received independent mathematical review or formal verification. The exact-arithmetic checks accompanying this document validate finite witnesses, not the probability theorems or novelty priority.

## 1. Protocol and quantities

Let `d >= 2`, `N >= 1`, `Q >= 1`, `Delta > 0`, and `B=Q Delta`. The public original class is

\[
\Theta=\{-Q,\ldots,Q\}^d,\qquad w=\Delta a,\qquad q=2Q+1.
\]

Features are the rows of `H in R^{N x d}`. The intact record is

\[
\ell_i=\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta(Ha)_i+\xi_i}{W}\right\rfloor,
\qquad W>0,\quad \xi\in[0,W)^N.
\tag{1}
\]

This is an exact, unsaturated quantizer. The encoder sees `(H,xi,ell,a)` and writes a protected certificate before any tamper. The decoder sees `(H,xi,ell,z,certificate)`. The adversary may replace at most one coordinate with any label in the alphabet, including after seeing the certificate. Recovery means exact recovery of `a`, simultaneously for every allowed tamper.

For a transcript, let `F_ell` be all originals producing it. Two distinct originals in this fiber are confusable iff they differ in at most two coordinates: they admit a shared one-change checkpoint. Hence a valid certificate is a proper coloring of this graph. Define `L*_ell` as the minimum fixed-length certificate budget and

\[
L^*_{\max}(H,\xi)=\max_{\ell:F_\ell\ne\varnothing}L^*_\ell.
\tag{2}
\]

The budget, prime, and all decoding conventions may depend on public `(H,xi,ell)`, but not on the unknown original through uncounted side information. All bits bounds below use fixed-length, or publicly padded, certificates. Encoding a color is an information-theoretic statement; the explicit upper construction avoids enumerating all originals.

For comparison with the previous coupled-row result, let `D_ell` be the nonzero label differences of all confusable pairs in `F_ell`. A modulus `p` has an **alias** when `D_ell` contains a nonzero vector in `(p Z)^d`. Its column-conflict graph `Gamma_p` has edge `{j,k}` when some difference has two nonzero residues satisfying `v_j+v_k=0 mod p`.

## 2. A deterministic upper bound requiring no union over originals

Define the two-sparse feature gain

\[
\gamma_2(H)=\inf_{\|u\|_2=1,\ |\operatorname{supp}u|\le2}\|Hu\|_\infty.
\tag{3}
\]

**Lemma 1 (uniform ambiguity bound).** If `gamma_2(H) >= lambda > 0`, every confusable pair in every transcript satisfies

\[
\|a-b\|_\infty < \frac{W}{\Delta\lambda}.
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** Equal half-open quantization cells imply `||Delta H(a-b)||_infty < W`. The difference is at most two-sparse. Equation (3) gives `Delta lambda ||a-b||_2 < W`, which implies (4). This uses neither a particular original nor random offsets. ∎

Set

\[
R=\min\{2Q,\lceil W/(\Delta\lambda)\rceil-1\}.
\tag{5}
\]

If `R=0`, zero certificate bits suffice. Otherwise choose a prime `p>R` and let

\[
r=\min\{s\ge1:1+p+\cdots+p^{s-1}\ge d\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Give the `d` columns distinct one-dimensional subspaces of `F_p^r`, with public nonzero representatives `b_j`, and save

\[
C(a)=\sum_j a_j b_j\pmod p.
\tag{7}
\]

**Lemma 2 (achievable budget).** This gives

\[
L^*_{\max}(H,\xi)\le\lceil r\log_2p\rceil.
\tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** A nonzero confusable difference has at least one nonzero residue because every coordinate has magnitude less than `p`. If it has one nonzero residue, its checksum is nonzero. If it has two, the corresponding representatives are linearly independent, so their combination is nonzero. Thus all confusable pairs are separated. Enumerate original candidates within one substitution of the damaged row, and retain the candidate with the intact record and checksum. Exactly one remains. ∎

For `p >= d-1`, two field elements suffice. In particular, when `p >= d`, one may use the familiar pair of checks `sum_j a_j` and `sum_j j a_j mod p`. For a full uniform-grid box, interval intersection and congruence testing under each changed-column hypothesis implements decoding in `O(Nd+rd)` arithmetic operations. The proof does not require solving the optimization (3): any certified positive lower bound on it suffices.

## 3. Explicit Gaussian upper bound

Assume now that all entries of `H` are independent standard Gaussians. This is an iid Gaussian **feature/design** model; it is not an assertion that arbitrary nonlinear random features or trained representations have independent entries.

Fix `0<delta<1`, and put

\[
\begin{split}
U&=\sqrt{2\ln(8Nd/\delta)},\\
J&=\lceil4\pi\sqrt2\,U\rceil,\\
M&=\binom d2 J,\qquad A=\ln(4M/\delta),\\
z&=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{A}{2N}\right),\qquad
\lambda=z-\frac14.
\end{split}
\tag{9}
\]

Use (9) when `A<N` and `lambda>0`. Otherwise it makes no positive claim; one can fall back to a data-independent alphabet bound.

**Lemma 3 (Gaussian sparse gain).** With probability at least `1-delta/2`, both

\[
\max_{i,j}|H_{ij}|\le U,
\qquad \gamma_2(H)\ge\lambda
\tag{10}
\]

hold. Consequently, (5)–(8) are valid simultaneously for all offsets in `[0,W)^N`, all transcripts, all originals, and all legal tampers.

**Proof.** A two-sided Gaussian tail bound and a union bound give

`P(max |H_ij|>U) <= 2Nd exp(-U^2/2) = delta/4`.

For every support pair choose `J` equally spaced unit directions on its circle. Each direction `v` has `N` independent standard normal projections. By the definition of `z`,

\[
\Pr\{\|Hv\|_\infty<z\}=(1-A/N)^N\le e^{-A}.
\]

There are `M` directions, so the chance that any has gain below `z` is at most `M exp(-A)=delta/4`. On the maximum-entry event, every pair-restricted row has Euclidean norm at most `sqrt(2) U`. A unit vector is within Euclidean distance at most `pi/J` of a net direction. Its projections differ from the net direction's by at most `sqrt(2) U pi/J <= 1/4`. Therefore its maximum absolute projection is at least `z-1/4`. Singleton supports are covered by any pair containing them. ∎

The counting term is now `log M = O(log d + log log(Nd/delta))`. There is no `d log q`, and not even a `log q` term in the gain bound. The price is the extreme-value rate: for polynomial `d` and fixed `delta`, `lambda` is asymptotic to `sqrt(2 ln N)`, not to `N`.

## 4. A matching obstruction: a row centered inside every recorded cell

The following deterministic observation explains why this slower rate is real.

**Lemma 4 (centered-cell family).** Suppose `max |H_ij| <= U` and a grid row `w_0=Delta a_0` satisfies

\[
W\ell_i+3W/8\le(Hw_0+\xi)_i\le W\ell_i+5W/8\quad\text{for every }i,
\tag{11}
\]

and every coordinate of `w_0` is at least `W/(8U)` from the boundary of `[-B,B]`. Define

\[
k=\left\lfloor\frac{W}{8U\Delta}\right\rfloor.
\tag{12}
\]

Every grid perturbation of `a_0` supported on at most two coordinates with coordinate magnitudes at most `k` belongs to the same transcript `ell`.

If `k>=1`, the following all hold:

1. A fixed pair of columns gives a clique of size `(2k+1)^2`.
2. The one-coordinate ball gives a clique of size `1+2dk`; all its members share the checkpoint `a_0`.
3. Every integer modulus `2 <= p <= 2k` has a genuine alias in this fiber.
4. For **every prime p**, `Gamma_p` is the complete graph on `d` columns.

In particular,

\[
L^*_\ell\ge\left\lceil\log_2\max\{(2k+1)^2,1+2dk\}\right\rceil.
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** Such a perturbation changes each output by at most `2U Delta k <= W/4`. Combining this with (11) keeps each output in `[W ell_i+W/8,W ell_i+7W/8]`, strictly inside its recorded cell. The box-margin assumption keeps all weights legal. Two members varying a fixed pair differ in at most two coordinates, proving the first clique. The one-coordinate ball also has pairwise distance at most two and a common checkpoint, proving the second.

For an alias choose two labels on one coordinate of the interval `[a_0j-k,a_0j+k]` separated by `p`. Their difference is `p e_j`. For a conflict edge use the difference between `a_0` and `a_0+e_j-e_k`. These two originals are in the fiber for every pair `j != k`. Their residues are nonzero and sum to zero for every prime, including `p=2`. Every clique member must have a distinct certificate, which proves (13). ∎

The pair clique does not need one checkpoint common to the entire clique; pairwise common checkpoints suffice for a coloring lower bound. This is why its size can exceed a single damaged-row candidate count.

### 4.1 Constructing the center on the finite grid

Assume `d>N` and set

\[
s=\sqrt d-\sqrt N-\sqrt{2\ln(8/\delta)} >0,
\qquad V=\frac{W\sqrt N}{2s}.
\tag{14}
\]

Impose the explicit mesh and range conditions

\[
\Delta U\sqrt{32d\ln(4N)}\le W,
\qquad B\ge V+\Delta+\frac{W}{8U}.
\tag{15}
\]

**Lemma 5 (uniform existence of a grid center).** With probability at least `1-delta/2` over `H`, for every offset vector and every original `w` satisfying

\[
\|w\|_\infty\le B-V-\Delta-W/(8U),
\tag{15a}
\]

its transcript has a center as in Lemma 4. Consequently, (13) and the alias and complete-graph conclusions hold for **each of these fibers**. In particular the zero original satisfies (15a) under (15), and its transcript is zero.

**Proof.** Along with the maximum-entry event, use the standard Gaussian smallest-singular-value bound on `H^T`: its smallest singular value is at least `s`, except on an event of probability at most `2 exp(-[2 ln(8/delta)]/2)=delta/4`. This is a bound on the `N` nonzero singular values, not on the kernel of `H`.

Fix any such original and offsets, and let `ell` be its transcript. Its displacement to the cell centers is `t=W(ell+1/2)-Hw-xi`, with `||t||_infty<=W/2`. Correct it using the minimum-norm solution:

\[
w_*=w+H^T(HH^T)^{-1}\left(W(\ell+\tfrac12\mathbf1)-Hw-\xi\right).
\tag{16}
\]

Then `Hw_*+xi = W(ell+1/2)` and `||w_*-w||_infty <= ||w_*-w||_2 <= V`.

Round each coordinate independently and without bias to one of its two adjacent grid values. The rounding errors `e_j` have mean zero and range length at most `Delta`; their magnitudes are at most `Delta`. Hoeffding's inequality, conditional on `H,w_*`, gives

\[
\Pr_{\rm round}\{|(He)_i|\ge W/8\}
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{W^2}{32\Delta^2\sum_jH_{ij}^2}\right)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{W^2}{32\Delta^2dU^2}\right).
\tag{17}
\]

By (15) a union bound over rows is at most `1/2`. Thus at least one grid rounding has all output errors less than `W/8`. Its coordinates have magnitude at most `||w||_infty+V+Delta`; (15a) supplies the margin required by Lemma 4. This is a deterministic existence argument for each original and offset vector once `H` obeys the two stated conditions. No union bound over originals or offsets, and no claim of one center working for different transcripts, is used. ∎

The Gaussian singular-value inequality used here is Corollary 5.35 of [Vershynin, *Introduction to the non-asymptotic analysis of random matrices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3027). The remaining construction and rounding argument are given above explicitly.

## 5. Finite-sample theorem and sharp regime

**Theorem 1 (uniform sample–certificate sandwich).** In the Gaussian feature model, assume the positivity conditions for (9) and (14), the mesh and range conditions (15), and define `R,p,r,k` as above. With probability at least `1-delta` over `H`, simultaneously for every offset vector,

\[
\boxed{
\left\lceil\log_2\max\{(2k+1)^2,1+2dk\}\right\rceil
\le L^*_{\max}(H,\xi)
\le\left\lceil r\log_2 p\right\rceil.
}
\tag{18}
\]

In fact the same lower and upper bounds hold with `L*_max` replaced by `L*_ell` for **every transcript containing an original satisfying (15a)**. If `k>=1`, every such fiber has all the alias and complete-column-graph obstructions of Lemma 4.

**Proof.** The maximum-entry, angular-net, and singular-value failure probabilities sum to at most `3delta/4 <= delta`. Intersect these events and apply Lemmas 1–5. Both sides refer to the same original class, arithmetic, tamper model, and uniform recovery requirement. ∎

**Corollary 1 (matching bits in the high-resolution regime).** Fix positive constants `eta,C` and a fixed confidence level. Consider growing `N,d` with

\[
(1+\eta)N\le d\le N^C,
\tag{19}
\]

and with (15) and `k>=d`. Then, for sufficiently large `N`, on the event of Theorem 1,

\[
L^*_{\max}(H,\xi)
=2\log_2(W/\Delta)-\log_2\ln N+O_{\eta,C,\delta}(1).
\tag{20}
\]

The same formula holds for `L*_ell` whenever the fiber contains an original satisfying (15a).

**Proof.** Here `U=Theta(sqrt(ln N))`. Also `A=O(ln N)` and standard Gaussian tail asymptotics give `lambda=Theta(sqrt(ln N))`. The lower pair clique has size `Theta((W/(Delta U))^2)`; floors only change universal constants since `k>=d>=2`.

For the upper bound, choose `p` as the smallest prime strictly exceeding `R`. The actual pair clique includes differences of size `2k`, so (4)–(5) imply `R>=2k>=2d` on the same event. Bertrand's bound supplies `p<=2R`, and therefore `r=2`. Since `R <= W/(Delta lambda)`, the upper bound is at most `2 log_2(W/(Delta lambda))+O(1)`. The ratio `U/lambda` is bounded under (19), so lower and upper differ by only a constant number of bits. ∎

The assumptions are nonempty: a sufficiently small mesh `Delta <= c W/(d sqrt(ln(Nd)))` gives `k>=d` and eventually the mesh condition; a sufficiently wide box with `B/W` depending only on `eta` gives the range condition. This is a high-resolution theorem, not a claim for every `q`.

For interpreting a sample-size curve at fixed dimension and precision, restrict `N` to a window `d^a <= N <= d/(1+eta)` with fixed `0<a<1`, and choose the mesh and range once to satisfy the conditions throughout that window. Equation (20) then describes that window with constants independent of `N`. It does not describe the eventual `N >> d` regime or the zero-bit transition at fixed finite alphabet.

The theorem removes the earlier exhaustive-class counting cost but rules out a `1/N` **uniform ambiguity** law in this regime. It also rules out a uniform theorem asserting that the chromatic number of the actual column-conflict graph decreases before `k` drops below one. A complete column graph does not by itself force a large arbitrary certificate; the matching model-graph cliques are what establish necessity.

## 6. Why this differs from the fixed-original picture

The centering witness is allowed to depend on `H,xi` because a uniform-fiber certificate must work for **every original consistent with the observed transcript**. The witness still belongs to the same public finite box fixed before sampling. We have not changed that class or selected a new alphabet after seeing the data. Lemma 5 starts with the transcript of any sufficiently interior original, even one fixed before sampling or selected by training. It then finds the hard clique elsewhere in that very same fiber.

This lower bound does not show that decoding the actual original is usually hard if the protocol may fail on other members of its fiber. For an original fixed independently of the observed data, random dither makes some recorded outputs very close to a cell boundary. For the alternative witness in (16), outputs are centered instead. More coordinates than recorded examples make that centering possible. Therefore small anchored polygons at the actual original do not imply a small uniform-fiber certificate.

The probability statements are distinct:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Uniform:}&\Pr_D\{\forall a,\ \forall z\text{ legal from }a,\ \operatorname{Dec}=a\}\ge1-\delta;\\
\text{Per-original:}&\forall a,\ \Pr_D\{\forall z\text{ legal from }a,\ \operatorname{Dec}=a\}\ge1-\delta.
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]

Both certificates are pre-tamper. Both allow an adaptive tamper after the certificate is revealed. Only the order of the original and data quantifiers changes. There is also an intermediate requirement: with high probability over the record of one fixed original, the certificate is valid for **all originals in its fiber**. Theorem 1's interior-fiber strengthening obstructs the fast rate even for this intermediate requirement. The per-original result below deliberately does not impose it. In that result, the fixed original is independent of the sampled features and dithers. Substituting a row trained on these same features is not justified by this proposition.

### 6.1 A rigorously faster per-original theorem

For completeness, the following bounded alternative isolates the cost of that quantifier choice. Take independent uniform dithers, independent Gaussian features, and a fixed original in the same grid box. Define

\[
\begin{split}
M_0&=2d(q-1)+4\binom d2(q-1)^2,\\
\kappa&=\Pr\{|G|\ge1\},\quad G\sim N(0,1),\\
\Lambda_0&=\ln(M_0/\delta),\qquad
t=\frac{W\Lambda_0}{\kappa N}.
\end{split}
\tag{22}
\]

Assume `N>=Lambda_0/kappa`. Set `R_0=min(2Q,ceil(t/Delta)-1)`, choose a prime `p>R_0`, and use (6)–(7). If `R_0=0`, omit the certificate.

**Proposition 2 (per-original sufficiency).** This single publicly specified encoder and decoder succeeds, for each fixed original, against every legal tamper with probability at least `1-delta`. The probability bound does not require a union over `q^d` originals.

**Proof.** For a numerical difference `v` and one random feature/dither pair, conditional on the feature the probability that the two quantized records differ is `min(1,|h dot v|/W)`. Since `h dot v` is Gaussian with standard deviation `||v||_2`, this probability is at least

`kappa min(1,||v||_infty/W)`.

There are at most `M_0` nonzero at-most-two-sparse differences from the fixed original to other grid originals. Each difference of infinity norm at least `t` survives all `N` records with probability at most `exp(-kappa N t/W)=exp(-Lambda_0)`. The condition on `N` ensures `t<=W`; larger differences satisfy at least the same detection bound. Union bounding only these differences gives failure at most `delta`.

On the good event, every confusable alternative to the actual original differs by at most `R_0` labels per coordinate. The checksum proof then separates the actual original from all such alternatives. Any competing original within one substitution of a legal damaged checkpoint is at most two substitutions from the actual original. Thus all legal tampers are repaired on this one event. Confusability among other originals is irrelevant for this weaker guarantee. ∎

This establishes achievability of a genuinely smaller budget; it does not label the gap computational. It is also not a guarantee conditioned on an arbitrary observed transcript.

**Proposition 3 (per-original necessity).** Suppose a deterministic protocol has a public worst-case fixed budget `L` and meets the per-original probability guarantee for every original. Take `0<delta<3/4`, put

\[
m=\mathbb E(|h_j|+|h_k|)=2\sqrt{2/\pi},\qquad
k_0=\min\left\{Q,\left\lfloor\frac{W}{8Nm\Delta}\right\rfloor\right\},\qquad K=(2k_0+1)^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Then

\[
2^L\ge(3/4-\delta)K.
\tag{24}
\]

**Proof.** Fix a pair of columns and the square of grid originals centered at zero with coordinate radius `k_0`. This set has `K` originals fixed before sampling, and every pair is confusable if their transcripts agree. Conditional on one feature row, all their numerical predictions lie in `[-s_i,s_i]`, where `s_i=Delta k_0(|H_ij|+|H_ik|)`. A uniform dither puts this entire interval inside the zero cell except with probability at most `2s_i/W` (the bound may exceed one). Taking expectations and a union bound over `N` gives probability at least `3/4` that the whole square has the same record.

For each realized dataset, call an original successful if the protocol repairs every allowed tamper from it. On the common-record event, at most `2^L` originals in the square can be successful: two successful originals with the same certificate would give contradictory answers on a shared checkpoint. On its complement, at most `K` can be successful. Thus the expected number of successful originals is at most `2^L+K/4`. The assumed per-original guarantee makes the same expectation at least `(1-delta)K`, proving (24). ∎

This lower bound is proved for the same per-original guarantee, rather than reusing a uniform clique bound without justification. It bounds a worst-case fixed budget, not expected length or only the budget on one selected transcript. In the unclipped, high-resolution regime with `p>=d-1`, Propositions 2–3 yield the common leading dependence `2 log_2(W/(N Delta))`, with an upper/lower gap of order `log log(dq)+log(1/delta)` bits, not a constant-gap theorem for all parameter scalings.

## 7. Scope for trained features and finite-precision models

Lemmas 1, 2 and 4 apply to deterministic feature matrices. The centering argument also extends to any full-row-rank matrix with a sufficiently well-conditioned right inverse and sufficiently fine parameter grid. Consequently, independence of Gaussian entries is not the conceptual source of the obstruction. It provides transparent probability bounds.

Conversely, specifying a training algorithm can radically restrict the admissible originals. For example, an exact real-valued zero-initialized minimum-norm linear fit lies in the row span of the training design. If a Gaussian `N x d` design has `N<=d-2`, that row span almost surely contains no nonzero two-sparse vector: for each support pair, the remaining `d-2` columns have full row rank, so a row-space vector supported on that pair must vanish. Therefore two admissible originals cannot share a one-change checkpoint, and zero certificate bits suffice information-theoretically from the subspace restriction alone. Parameter rounding changes this argument.

This is one reason not to assert a universal trained-feature sample–bits law without fixing the training class and stored arithmetic. Repair using training-induced redundancy already has important prior art: [Gao and Lafferty, *Model Repair: Robust Recovery of Over-Parameterized Statistical Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09912). That paper studies repair using design information and develops linear, random-feature, and neural-model results. The protected-bit tradeoff here needs a separate novelty comparison; “overparameterization enables repair” is not a new claim.

The quantization geometry also has substantial prior art. [Jacques, Hammond and Fadili, *Dequantizing Compressed Sensing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.2367) study Gaussian measurement geometry with non-Euclidean residual constraints. [Jacques, *Error Decay of (almost) Consistent Signal Estimations from Quantized Gaussian Random Projections*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0022) proves fast consistency-error rates for low-complexity original classes. Our originals are dense and unconstrained within the box; only differences between confusable originals are sparse. Thus those sparse-original guarantees do not directly imply a fast uniform-fiber repair law here. The sparse-gain upper bound and the modular checks should not individually be presented as novel discoveries. The candidate contribution is their combination with a finite-grid, same-transcript centering obstruction and matching protected-bit law. A focused priority check remains necessary.

The present result does not certify the Llama numbers, assume a global uniform BF16 lattice, cover saturation, or allow uncharged per-original calibration data. Under a magnitude cap on tampering, the upper bound remains safe. For the lower construction one can replace `W/(8U)` by `min(W/(8U),rho/2)` so every pair-clique common-checkpoint tamper is legal; the sharp regime then additionally requires the cap not to truncate that radius.

## 8. Research decision

The specific hoped-for statement—rapid uniform shrinkage of alias complexity and column-conflict complexity in an overparameterized full-grid random-feature model—should not be pursued unchanged. Theorem 1 supplies a concrete obstruction and a matching upper bound in a meaningful regime.

There are two clean stories, not a need to add many technical branches:

* **Keep the previous uniform-fiber requirement.** The central result is that samples save only logarithm-of-logarithm bits in the stated overparameterized regime, even for transcripts from fixed interior originals, despite fast local ambiguity decay. The complete conflict graph and centered-cell clique explain why. This is a substantial correction to the earlier intuition, but its conference-level novelty still needs focused prior-art checking and independent proof review.
* **Use a fixed-original statistical requirement.** Pre-tamper repair can then have the faster sample–bits law. Propositions 2–3 give a rigorous baseline. Closing their remaining logarithmic gap, or justifying independence through held-out feature observations, would be a separate, explicitly scoped next step. The present proof does not cover an arbitrary model trained on the same observations.

No additional large code repository is needed to assess these theorems. Before extending to trained features, the essential missing specification is what information the repair protocol has about the training algorithm and which originals it must protect. That choice should precede another experiment campaign.
